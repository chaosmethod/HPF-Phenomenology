# Lattice Mirror Symmetry (LMS)

### HPF Supporting Document: Lattice Mirror Symmetry (LMS)

## 1. Executive Summary
Lattice Mirror Symmetry (LMS) identifies quantum entanglement as a single informational structure viewed from dual lattice angles. This provides a non-local, yet local-substrate, explanation for Einstein’s "spooky action". It derives the factor of $1/2$ in the gravitational metric as a direct consequence of an observer perceiving a primary projection and its lattice reflection simultaneously.

## 2. The Mirror Mechanism
Under Axiom II (Reversible Lattice Updates), all updates must be bijective. LMS treats entangled pairs not as separate entities, but as a single execution mirrored across the lattice.
* **Substrate Unity:** Entangled particles share the same Scheduler Time ($t_{sched}$) and update logic.
* **Coordinate Duality:** Perceived spatial separation is a projection effect caused by differing "lattice angles" within the 3D manifold.
* **Update Bijectivity:** State changes are instantaneous across the projection because the Bijective Update ($U_i$) acts on a shared information core at the substrate level.

## 3. Derivation of the $1/2$ Factor
The $1/2$ factor in the weak-field metric ($g_{tt} \approx 1 + \frac{1}{2}\frac{R_s}{r}$) emerges from the observational split between direct and reflected data.

### The Metric Relation
The rate of coherent proper time ($\tau$) is regulated by update availability ($\alpha$):
$$\frac{d\tau(x)}{dt_{sched}} \propto \sqrt{\alpha(x)}$$

In a mirrored system, the total load is partitioned between the two observable instances:

| Component | Source | Impact on Proper Time ($\tau$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Direct View** | Primary Lattice Projection | $1/2$ of total perceived update pressure |
| **Reflected View** | Entangled Lattice Mirror | $1/2$ of total perceived update pressure |
| **Combined Metric** | Sum of Observables | $\gamma_{HPF} = 1 + \frac{1}{2}u(r)$ |

## 4. Legality and Decoherence
Maintaining the mirror-image update across the lattice is an active execution cost.
* **Entanglement Load ($L_{ent}$):** This consumes a portion of the local update capacity ($\alpha$).
* **Saturation Trigger:** When the local entropy flux $S_f$ exceeds the threshold $\lambda \approx 5.79$, the scheduler can no longer sustain the mirrored state.
* **Mandatory Handoff:** To preserve fundamental reversibility, HPF forces state partitioning, leading to the loss of non-local correlation (Decoherence).

## 5. Falsifiability
* **Stochastic Jitter:** Timing of correlations should exhibit a noise floor proportional to $\sqrt{N} L_{vac}$.
* **Kernel Shape:** Arrival-time residuals in high-compactness events should scale as $(1-u)^{-1/2}$, confirming the Coherent Latency model.
